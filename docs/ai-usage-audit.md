# WasteVoice AI — AI Usage Audit

## Purpose

This document records the role of AI as a development and ideation co-pilot. It does not turn AI-generated text into project evidence.

## Design-thinking use

AI was used as a divergence partner during the ideation stage to generate concepts across the required directions:

1. Sense
2. Predict
3. Understand language
4. Optimise
5. Detect anomalies
6. Assist a user
7. Automate a step
8. Make an invisible problem visible

Concepts were then compared using impact, technical feasibility, data availability, cost/sustainability, and ethics/privacy/risk.

## Development/documentation use

AI assistance was used to help structure software requirements, UI/workflow ideas, documentation, validation checklists and implementation reasoning.

## Verification rule

AI suggestions were treated as drafts. Project-specific facts were retained only when supported by field evidence, stakeholder evidence, repository implementation or an actual test.

## Rejected or bounded AI claims

The project explicitly rejects unsupported claims such as:

- AI automatically solves campus waste accumulation.
- AI prediction is accurate before sufficient historical data exists.
- An image proves cleaning completion without human review.
- AI can independently assign or resolve operational work.

## Hallucination-control principle

For the future report-structuring feature, missing information should remain unknown rather than being invented. The reporter must review and correct AI suggestions.

## Academic integrity

AI is treated as a co-pilot rather than the author of the project's field observations, user evidence or final conclusions. Students remain responsible for understanding and defending the implementation and claims submitted for evaluation.
